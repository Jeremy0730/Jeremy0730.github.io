Why I want to contribute to DICOM learning

18 October 2026 · 6 min read · EN / 中文

I started building DR workstation software in 2017. In 2019 I moved to MG workstations and stayed with that work for years. Through both roles, DICOM was not a side topic. It was the language the devices spoke, and the language I had to learn carefully enough to ship software other people could trust.

At the beginning I thought DICOM was mainly a file format: open an image, read some tags, display a pixel matrix. That was only the entrance. Later came the network side of the standard, and with it a long list of ideas that refuse to stay in one neat box: Sequence, LUT, GSPS, Presentation Context, Abstract Syntax, Transfer Syntax, Structured Report, Association Negotiation. Each one makes sense once you have lived with it. Together they feel endless.

The Chinese DICOM community already has people who carried this load before me. I learned step by step from writers such as zssure, whose posts once made a dense standard feel approachable. Their work matters. It also seems quieter now than it used to. That gap is part of why I want to add my own small share, rather than wait for someone else to resume the trail.

I should be clear about what I will not do. I am not translating the DICOM standard. Engineers who need to implement against it should read the official text themselves. That document is the gold source: the most accurate reference, and the only one that settles an argument when implementations disagree. A secondary translation cannot replace it, and I will not pretend otherwise.

What I will try to do is different. I want to organize the concepts into a usable map—something technical people and non-technical people can both hold in mind before they go deeper. A salesperson, a product manager, a new developer, and a tired debug session all need a shared skeleton of what DICOM is about. After that map, the standard becomes less like fog and more like a place you can navigate. Alongside the writing, I also plan to publish small practical DICOM tools: the kind of utilities that save a morning when you are checking a tag, a transfer syntax, or a negotiation detail.

I am not chasing a reputation as an expert. I only want to leave a little help for people who are still struggling on this road—the same road I walked when the file format turned into a network of ideas. If one clearer framework, or one small tool, shortens that struggle, the work is already worth doing.

中文

为什么我想为 DICOM 学习社区做一点贡献

我从 2017 年开始做 DR 工作站软件开发，2019 年跳槽后继续做 MG 工作站软件。这些年里，DICOM 从来不是边角知识。它是设备之间说话的语言，也是我必须学到足以交付可靠软件的那一层基础。

刚接触时，我以为 DICOM 主要是一种文件格式：打开图像、读标签、显示像素。那其实只是入口。后来才慢慢碰到网络协议这一侧，以及一长串很难塞进同一个框里的概念：Sequence、LUT、GSPS、Presentation Context、Abstract Syntax、Transfer Syntax、Structured Report、Association Negotiation。单独看，每一个都能讲通；合在一起，却像没有尽头。

中文 DICOM 社区里，其实早有人在扛这件事。我当年就是靠 zssure 这类博主的文章，一步一步学起来的。他们的贡献很重要。只是这些年更新似乎少了。正因为如此，我更想凭自己的一己之力补上一点力量，而不是等别人重新把路走热。

有一件事需要先说清楚：我不会去做 DICOM 标准的翻译。技术人员若要真正落地实现，应当自行阅读 DICOM 标准原文。那是最准确、也最不容易走偏的金标准；实现细节起争议时，最终也只能回到原文。二手翻译替代不了它，我也不会假装能替代。

我真正想做的，是另一件事。把知识概念梳理成一张能用的地图，让技术人员和非技术人员都能先有一个大体框架，再决定往哪里深挖。销售、产品、新人工程师，以及凌晨还在排查协商问题的人，都需要同一具关于 DICOM 的骨架。有了骨架，标准就少一点像雾，多一点像可以走路的地方。除了文字，我也会陆续输出一些 DICOM 实用小工具——那种在核对标签、传输语法或协商细节时，能省掉一个上午的小东西。

我不追求成为大佬。我只想为还在这条路上挣扎的人，提供一点学习上的帮助——就像当年的我，从“它是一个文件”走到“它是一整套系统之间的协议”。如果一张更清楚的知识框架，或一个小工具，能让那段挣扎短一点，这件事就已经值得做。

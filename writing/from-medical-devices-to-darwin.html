From medical devices to Darwin

8 October 2026 · 4 min read

I spent six years building software for medical devices at Sino Medical Device Technology, a company later acquired by Haier Group. The work sat close to the hardware: Windows desktop applications in C++ and C#, prototypes that had to be tested, and features that only counted if a technician could trust them on a real machine.

Before that I studied biomedical engineering at Southern Medical University in Guangzhou. The degree taught me to respect the gap between a diagram and a device in someone’s hands. Software was the part of that gap I kept coming back to.

In 2025 I moved to Darwin and started a Master of Information Technology in Software Engineering at Charles Darwin University. The course pulls me through database systems, cybernetics, and IT infrastructure, next to the engineering practice I already have. I am eligible for work-integrated learning, and I want that time spent on real systems.

Darwin moves differently from a device lab. I now work at Phone Planet, diagnosing hardware and software faults and explaining them in plain language. The problems are smaller than a medical-device release. The skill is the same: find the fault, say what it is, and leave the person better off than when they walked in.

This site is where I keep notes on that path. Software engineering, project delivery, and the ordinary work of making tools other people can actually use.
